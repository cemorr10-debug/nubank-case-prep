Yield strategy & mass-market pricing · Q3

What 13% actually costs.

The five numbers I would put on the table

1
What the next peso of deposit costs7.00% base · 13% premium, capped at $25,000 and gated on one purchase
2
What it earns6.49% parked in CETES · roughly 25% lent, net of losses (my assumption)
3
How many pesos leave per point cutYou already ran this experiment. On 5 January you cut 200bp, 15% → 13%. The balance response by segment is sitting in our data. I do not have it. That analysis is week one.
4
How much of it we can lend35% today · the group runs near 58% (assumption)
5
What retention costs per leveryield · progression · Nu+ at roughly 0.3%

The cap is doing the work

13% is not our cost of funds. It is a capped, self-liquidating acquisition budget sitting on the first $25,000. Above the cap we pay 7.00%, which is TIIE de fondeo exactly.

Ceiling on the true subsidy: $1,500 per customer per year: 600bp over TIIE de fondeo, on a capped base. That is the acquisition budget. The question is not whether it is too big. It is what we make people do to earn it.
Which reframes the whole debate: we are not overpaying for deposits. We are paying a bounded bounty for a behaviour, and right now the behaviour we buy is a $20 coffee.

Yield strategy & mass-market pricing · Q3

Don't cut the rate. Re-gate it.

DecisionOption AOption BThe call
Headline rateCut it. Saves cost of funds · loses balances, and buys a bad headline every timeHold it, re-gate it. Same spend, pointed at deployable incomeRe-gate. Same budget, better peso.
Premium gateOne purchase a month. Proves alive, not banking with usIncome landing here. Turns an interest expense into a funding-mix changeIncome. That is the whole strategy.
The capFlat $25,000. Overpays the rate-shopping tailOne month of income. Underpays large depositors, who are the least loyal money we holdIncome cap. Take the tail risk.
How it is quoted"13% fija". We own a number we must defend, and every move is our announcementCETES + 6.5. Transmission is automatic whenever the curve moves. Brazil already quotes 120% of CDISpread. México is the outlier.

Why spread-quoting, honestly

Not because it "falls with Banxico": consensus has the policy rate flat at 6.50% through 2027, so in the base case it falls with nothing. The real reasons: we stop owning a number we have to defend, transmission is automatic if and when the curve does move, and it is the group's existing convention.

If I argued this on rate cuts that are not coming, the first analyst in the room would take the slide apart, and they would be right.

The rule I would hold the team to

·
Pay premium only where it buys income we can lend against.
·
Price risk in the line, never in the rate.
·
Buy retention with progression before yield.Our own history says the base was never bought with rate: Cuenta Nu launched at 9% under an 11.25% policy rate.
·
CAT goes on the scorecard.A CAT near 85% is what lending blind costs. Visibility is what makes credit cheaper, so that number should fall as visibility rises, rather than be defended.
Responsible AI & personalisation · Q7

Personalisation may only move a price in the customer's favour.

This section is not bolted on. Tiering, conditionality and income-sized limits are personalised pricing. The fairness question is generated by the pricing design on the last two slides, so it has to be answered by the same person.

The line I will not cross

Elasticity tracks desperation.

In a price-sensitive, low-income base, willingness-to-pay is highest where the alternatives are worst. Optimising for it charges the most to the people least able to bear it, and it is legal, effective, and exactly how a mission dies quietly.

So AI sets eligibility, limits and timing. Never "this customer will tolerate worse."
How it gets tested

Fairness on outcomes, not on inputs.

Dropping gender from a model does not make it fair. Proxies survive. So we test the outputs: approval rate, limit and price parity by gender and by state, run as standing monitoring, not a launch gate.

Exclusion by policy on the input side too: no phone, contacts or location, ever, regardless of lift.
What the customer sees

Our estimate, before we price on it.

Beto sees the income we think he has, the reasons, a "that's not right" button, and a person to appeal to. If we cannot explain a decision to the customer, the model is using something it should not be.

We are a supervised bank now. Build the appeal path before the model, not after CONDUSEF asks.
The cheapest fairness control we have is the one on slide seven: same tier, same price, both doors. A single price removes the entire surface where personalised pricing could go wrong, and it costs us return per peso, not customers. I would rather defend a smaller line than a higher rate.
First six months, then 24 · Q8 & Q2

Ordered by what expires first.

Only one thing here has a clock on it: the licence advantage. Anything a licensed competitor could also do goes later. Anything only we can do right now goes first.

Months 0–6 · my top three priorities
1
Fix what we measure about deposits.Pull the January cut apart by segment. Balance sensitivity via a geographic holdout, MDE pre-registered. Re-quote yield as a spread. Retire "deposit growth" as a goal. First because it is nearly free and it is the evidence base for the other two.
2

3
Stand up Beto's spine.Consent flow, income estimator, fairness harness. Not the whole product. Third because it has the longest lead time, so it has to start early to land late.
Months 7–12

Beto's door to general availability at the same tier · credit lines sized on observed income · the bundle live end to end · protection attached.
Measured on landings, and on lending moving off 35%.
Months 13–24

Turn the anchor into depth

Investments, newly licensable · préstamo timed to the quincena at scale · higher-value lending · and only now an employer-side offer.
Leading with an HR sales motion is how a challenger loses this market to people who have sold to HR for thirty years.

If the window is six months instead of twelve, because Mercado Pago is licensed sooner, H1 does not compress. H3 gets cut. I would rather have 300,000 salaries landing than a signed employer pipeline.

Alignment without authority · Q6 & Q8

I would not try to win the argument. I would change what we are arguing about.

Say Card's roadmap collides with a bundle I have to ship on a regulatory clock. I own none of the three teams that have to move.

With Credit Card

Trade an asset, not a favour.

I bring something they cannot get anywhere else: income they can underwrite against, and collection timed to payday. Both land on their delinquency line. In exchange I get placement in their flows. That is a trade with a number attached, not a request.
With Account · GBA

Own the spec, not the surface.

They own the account experience; I own what the tier promises and what the customer is worth. The interface between us is one shared customer-level P&L, so we argue about which sequence makes the customer worth more, which evidence can settle, instead of whose roadmap wins, which it cannot.
With global · representing México

Not an exception. The next instance.

Croma is the group's newest bundle: a tiered offer anchored on spend, for customers from R$5,000 a month. Right architecture, wrong anchor for this market. México's mass market needs the same architecture income-anchored, and that is the instance every newly licensed market will need first. I am not asking for a local exception. I am volunteering to build the version Colombia inherits.
Three things I would do before I needed to. Agree the shared P&L with Diego and both leads while nothing is at stake · get Legal to frame the regulatory date as a shared compliance exposure, so it arrives as a fact rather than as my deadline · and put the disagreement into a test design whose guardrail metric the other team chooses. And if Card still cannot move: ship the tier without the card component and add it later. Shipping on time beats winning the argument.
