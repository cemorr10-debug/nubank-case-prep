The customer problem

Two people who earn steadily, and a bank that can't tell.

Lupita, 29 nómina

"My salary goes to the bank my boss picked. I pay fees, I keep a minimum, and my credit line is whatever the bureau says — not what I actually earn."

She already lives in Nu: card, Cajitas, recargas. Her paycheck leaves the other bank within 48 hours — mostly to pay her Nu card.

Don Beto, 47 remesas

"My son sends money the last week of every month. Eleven months now, never late. Plus the tienda. But when I ask for credit, they look at me like I have nothing."

The most regular income in his family is invisible to every lender in the country.
Their income is invisible to the bank that would lend to them — so they pay more and get less. Fix what the bank can see, and both of them get the price and the credit their income deserves.

The idea

See them. Price them fairly. Grow them. Repeat.

The better Nu can see what you earn, the better price and the bigger line it can give you. And the longer you stay, the better it sees you.

A rate is copyable in a week. A year of someone's income landing with you is not. That is the moat — for the customer, it just feels like being understood.

A bank that sees you is a bank you keep.

Lupita's door · Formal

Trae tu nómina.

1

Portability is her legal right — free, eleven business days, and her employer never has to know. The incumbent's grip is an HR contract signed years ago. She just walks around it.

2

Everything else is already here.

Her card already pays itself from her Nu account. She already picks the date. Her Nu+ points already land in a Cajita. Her recargas are already here.

Only the paycheck is missing — and with it, knowing when payday is.
3

Her card pays itself the day after payday.

Salary lands on the 15th; card debits on the 16th. Fewer misses for her, lower losses for Card. Crédito de nómina economics — through data, not a contract with her employer.

What we count: her salary actually landing — never the request. The eleven-day wait is where she'd get nervous, so we name the date, show the steps and watch the old account for her. That is the product, not polish.

What raises his line

"Two more on-time payments, and your remesa landing here two more months, and your line goes to $19,500." Shown before he has to ask. A commitment, not a score.

I shipped this in México, to this segment: −1.43% default rate showing the next limit 10 days before it was due. A simple ladder captured most of it — ship the simple version first.

Why Nu can go where no bank has: people want to bring Nu their money. Aspiration gets Beto in the door; data lets us say yes. A risky segment gets a clear path instead of a no.

What it changes across credit and account

Seeing income is the input every product was missing.

1
A card limit that matches your salary.Not a bureau score. More yeses on thin files, bigger limits, fewer losses — Card's P&L, fed by Core's signal.
2
A card that pays itself the day after payday.Autopay and a chosen due date already exist. Knowing payday is the one missing input — and it is the one that moves delinquency.
3
A loan that fits the quincena.Préstamo de nómina, repaid the day after the salary lands, from the account it lands in. BBVA's and Banorte's payroll-loan moat — reached through data, not an employer contract.
4
A line you can see how to raise.Same tier for observed income. Progression buys loyalty cheaper than yield does — the customer who is climbing is the one who is repaying.

How we bring it to people

"Tu dinero llega aquí."

The promise

Not a rate. "Trae tu nómina — y llévate la línea que tu sueldo merece."

Every competitor advertises a number. We advertise what the number unlocks: a line sized on your salary, a card that pays itself, your money the day it lands, insured by the IPAB.
Sell the credit line, not the interest rate.

How we'll know — and where it breaks

One north star. Three checkpoints. Three risks.

North star: the share of customers whose income lands at Nu. Today ~8% (my assumption). Every other number moves because this one does.
