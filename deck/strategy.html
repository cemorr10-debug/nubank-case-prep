The customer problem

Two people. One problem.

Lupita, 29 formal

Her salary lands at the bank her employer picked years ago. She pays fees, keeps a minimum balance, and her credit line is set by a bureau score — not by the salary sitting in the account.

She uses Nu for everything else. The money leaves her payroll bank within 48 hours, mostly to pay a Nu card.

Don Beto, 47 informal

A remittance from his son in Texas arrives the last week of every month — eleven months running, varying under 4%. Plus cash from the tienda.

No bank will lend against any of it. The most regular income in his family is invisible.
Their income is invisible to the bank that would lend to them. So they pay more, and get less. That is the whole problem — for both of them.

The idea

See the customer. Price them fairly. Grow them. Repeat.

More accurate data about the customer → better prices and benefits → the bank people love.

Every turn of the loop makes the next decision better and the next competitor's worse. A rate is copyable in a week. A year of someone's income landing with you is not.

A bank that sees you is a bank you keep.

Door A · Formal

Trae tu nómina.

1

The wedge is portability.

It is the worker's legal right: free, eleven business days, and no employer conversation. The incumbent moat is an HR contract signed years ago. Portability routes around it.

2

The loop is already built.

Autopay already debits the Nu account. The customer already picks a due date. Points already route into Cajitas. Recargas are already in the app.

Only the paycheck is missing — and with it, knowing when payday is.
3

Payday-aligned autopay.

Debit the day after the salary lands. Fewer missed payments, on Card's P&L. Crédito de nómina economics — without employer deduction, through data instead of a contract.

The metric is landings, never requests. The deposit arriving is the event that changes the balance sheet. The eleven-day gap is where the funnel leaks — naming the date and watching the old account is the retention mechanic, not polish.

The path

Score the customer as if they had repaid on time. Show the limit that unlocks, and the two actions that get there. A commitment, not a score.

Shipped in México, this segment: −1.43% default rate exposing the next limit 10 days pre-due (ITT, 95% CI −2.00 to −0.90). A plain loan-number ladder captured ~4/5 of it — ship the simple version first.

Why Nu can go deeper than anyone: aspiration is the acquisition channel — people want to bring Nu their money — and data is the underwriting channel. A risky segment gets a clear path instead of a no.

What could go wrong

Five risks, and what I would do about each.

1
Balance sensitivity rises once Mercado Pago and Klar are licensed. most likelyIt is low today because the base was won on brand — Cuenta Nu launched at 9% under an 11.25% policy rate. Measure it by segment in the first six weeks, before anyone else is a bank.
2
The eleven-day portability gap leaks.Name the date, show the steps, watch the old account. That is the product, not polish.
3
Bust-out on newly sized lines. creditStage limits, short tenors, progression rather than jumps. Three populations — fraud, over-reached, climbers — and the third has to outweigh the first two. Measure that, don't assume it.
4
Proxies creep into the income model. reputationalExclusion by policy, outcome parity, and a counterfactual you can safely show the customer. If you can't show it, the model is using something it shouldn't.
5
We are a supervised bank now.CNBV model-risk governance and CONDUSEF explainability apply from day one. Build the appeal path before the model, not after. And treat Rollee / Palenca as one signal among four — never the gate.
