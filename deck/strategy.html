The customer problem

Two people. Two different problems.

Lupita, 29 nómina

"My salary goes to the bank my boss picked. I pay fees, I keep a minimum, and my credit line is whatever the bureau says — not what I earn."

She already lives in Nu — card, Cajitas, recargas. Her paycheck leaves the other bank within 48 hours, mostly to pay her Nu card.
Her problem is plumbing. The paycheck is parked in the wrong place.

Don Beto, 47 remesas

"My son sends money the last week of every month — eleven months, never late. Plus the tienda. When I ask for credit they look at me like I have nothing." So he borrows the way people without a bank borrow: creatively, and at high risk.

Lupita's problem is solvable today — the licence and payroll do it. Beto's is a loop, and it is where the informal track earns its place: the product, the price and the path all have to change at once, or none of them holds.

The idea

See them. Price them fairly. Grow them. Repeat.

Seeing income answers can they repay. A visible path answers will they. Price on both, and the loop runs the right way.

Every turn makes the next decision better and the next competitor's worse. A rate is copyable in a week. A year of someone's income landing with you is not.

A bank that sees you is a bank you keep.

2

Her card already pays itself from her Nu account. She already picks the date. Her Nu+ points already land in a Cajita. Only the paycheck is missing — and with it, knowing payday.

Salary lands the 15th; card debits the 16th. That captures the timing advantage of payroll lending without an employer contract — though it is a debit she controls, not a deduction at source, so I would size the gain from a test rather than assume it.

Beto's door · Informal

Fix the product, the price and the path — together.

Product fit

One line that grows. No stacking.

His remesa and deposit rhythm are the income. We size one line to what we can see, with confidence attached — and it grows with his record instead of him juggling four lenders.

The spine is first-party — his remesa and his deposits land in a Nu account, needing no open-banking regime, which México still has not published. Rollee / Palenca are an optional third signal, never the pipe. Never his phone, contacts or location — by policy.
Price fit

Same price as Lupita. Risk lives in the line, not the rate.

A higher rate for the customer with less paperwork is what makes borrowing emergency-only. So we hold the price and size the line to our confidence. Same rate — not the same return per peso, and I take that hit early, because shorter tenors and progression push risk down over the relationship.

The rule for AI in pricing: personalisation may only move a price in the customer's favour. It sets eligibility, limits and timing — never that this customer will tolerate worse. Elasticity tracks desperation, so optimising for willingness-to-pay charges the most to those least able to bear it.
He sees our estimate before we price him on it, with the reasons and a person to appeal to. Fairness tested on outcomes — approval, limit and price parity by gender and state.
Pathway fit

See exactly what raises the line.

"Two more on-time payments, your remesa landing two more months, and your line goes to $19,500." Shown before he asks. In thin-file lending the good customers leave as fast as the bad ones default — because nobody gives them a reason to stay.

"I want to see my name next to a higher limit. It motivates me to work harder at my job too."A customer, on a growth hub I shipped in México for this segment. It cut default 1.43% (ITT) — and a simple ladder captured most of it.

Budget-neutral at launch. Base = CETES matched (today's 6.50%). Premium = CETES + 6.5 (today's 13%). What changes is who earns the premium — a card swipe today, income landing tomorrow — not how much premium is spent. Every base component already exists in the app; the licence adds payroll hosting, IPAB cover, higher limits and investments.

One tier, two doors — deliberately. Two bundles would mean two roadmaps, two pricing committees, and a worse deal for the customer with less paperwork.

Yield strategy & mass-market pricing

How I'd actually make the pricing call.

The five numbers on the table

What the next peso of deposit costs6.50% base · 13% premium (≈CETES + 6.5), capped at MX$25k

What it earns6.49% parked in CETES · ~25% lent, net of losses (mine)
3
How many pesos leave per point cutβ≈2.9 — the 15→11.5 move kept >90% of balances
4
How much we can lend35% today · 60% once income is visible

What retention costs per lever13% yield · progression · Nu+ at ~0.3%

On the price itself: our personal loan averages a CAT near 85%, because it is priced for what we cannot see. Visibility is what makes credit cheaper — so CAT goes on the scorecard and should fall as visibility rises, rather than be defended.

The rule: pay premium only where it buys income we can lend against · price risk in the line, not the rate · buy retention with progression before yield. Budget-neutral at launch — what changes is who earns it. Every input is a slider in the live model; the base was never bought with rate — Cuenta Nu launched at 9% under an 11.25% policy rate.

Strategy & sequencing · 24 months

Ordered by what expires first.

Only one thing has a clock on it: the licence advantage. Anything a licensed competitor could also do goes later. Anything only we can do right now goes first.

H1 · months 0–6 · my first three priorities
1
Fix what we measure about deposits.Balance sensitivity by segment via a geographic holdout, MDE pre-registered. Re-quote yield as a spread. Retire "deposit growth" as a goal. First because it is nearly free and it is the evidence base for the other two.
2
Ship Lupita's door to the cohort we can already see.Portability for customers whose payroll pattern is already in our data. Second because it is the thing that expires.
3
Stand up Beto's spine.Consent flow, income estimator, fairness harness. Not the whole product. Third because it has the longest lead time, so it has to start early to land late.
H2 · months 7–12

Scale the anchor before the window closes

Beto's door to general availability at the same tier · credit lines sized on observed income · the bundle live end-to-end · protection attached.
Measured on landings and on lending moving off 35%.
H3 · months 13–24

Turn the anchor into depth

Investments — newly licensable · préstamo timed to the quincena at scale · higher-value lending · and only now an employer-side offer.
Leading with an HR sales motion is how a challenger loses this market to people who have sold to HR for thirty years.

If the window is six months instead of twelve — because Mercado Pago is licensed sooner — H1 does not compress. H3 gets cut. I would rather have 300,000 salaries landing than a signed employer pipeline.

Three org calls I would defend

1
Hire the pricing analyst before the second PM.The bottleneck here is decision quality, not shipping throughput. One more PM ships more things; one analyst makes the yield decision right, and that decision is worth more than a quarter of roadmap.
2
Do not ask to own Card's or GBA's surfaces.Own the income signal and the customer-level P&L instead. Asking for org control costs six months and the relationship — and I would lose the argument anyway.
3
Put the designer on Beto's door, not Lupita's.Portability is a known pattern. Consent, an income estimate shown back, and a visible ladder are where craft decides whether it works at all.

One of two open roles, and the first PM reporting into this structure — how the pod is shaped now is how it scales.

Alignment without authority

I would not try to win the argument. I would change what we are arguing about.

Say Card's roadmap collides with a bundle I need to ship on a regulatory clock. I own none of the three teams that have to move.

With Credit Card

Trade an asset, not a favour.

I bring something they cannot get elsewhere: income they can underwrite against, and collection timed to payday. Both land on their delinquency line. They give me placement in their flows. That is a trade with a number on it — not a request.
With Account · GBA

Own the spec, not the surface.

They own the account experience; I own what the bundle promises and what the customer is worth. The interface between us is one shared customer-level P&L — so we argue about which sequence makes the customer worth more, which evidence can settle, instead of whose roadmap wins, which it cannot.
With global

Not an exception — the next instance.

Croma is the group's newest bundle: a platinum card, streaming cashback, telco, for customers from R$5,000 a month. Right architecture, spend-anchored. México's mass market needs the same architecture income-anchored — and that is the instance every newly licensed market will need first.
Three things I would do before I needed to. Agree the shared P&L with Diego and both leads while nothing is at stake · get Legal to frame the regulatory date as a shared compliance exposure, so it arrives as a fact rather than as my deadline · and put the disagreement into a test design with a guardrail metric the other team chooses. And if Card still cannot move: ship the bundle without the card component and add it later. Shipping on time beats winning the argument.
