See the customer clearly, and you can price them fairly.

The customer problem

Two people, one problem: the bank cannot see how they earn.

Lupita, 29 · formal nómina at a big bank

1
Her salary lands at BBVA.Because her employer chose it years ago. She pays fees, keeps a minimum, and her credit line is set by a bureau score, not by the salary in the account.
2
She already uses Nu for everything else.Card, Cajitas, recargas. The money leaves her payroll bank within 48 hours — mostly to pay a Nu card.
Nu holds her relationship. The other bank holds her paycheck.

Don Beto, 47 · informal no payslip

1
Money arrives every month.A remittance from his son in Texas in the last week of the month, eleven months running, varying under 4%. Plus cash from the tienda.
2
No bank will lend against it.He is one of 33.1M informal workers. Only 37% of Mexican adults can access formal credit at all — so the most regular income in his family is invisible.
His income is more regular than most salaries. Nobody is looking.

The thesis

More accurate data about the customer → better prices and benefits → the bank people love.

1
See.Salary via portability, or income inferred from remittance and deposit rhythm. Both are proof.
2
Price on what you see.Yield premium and credit line attached to income landing — not to a card swipe, not to a bureau score.
3
Show the path.Every customer sees what raises their line. Progression, not a black box.
4
They stay, and bring more.Leaving costs them their line. A rate never did that. More data, better prices, again.
A bank that sees you is a bank you keep.
Recommended solution

One tier. Two doors in.

Track A · Formal

Documented income — the paycheck comes to Nu
Direct deposit via portabilidadPayday-aligned autopayWallet & bill set-up (domiciliación)Salary available the day it landsLine sized on the salary

Worker-initiated, free by law, 11 business days, no employer conversation. The incumbent moat is an HR contract; portability routes around it.

Track B · Informal

Observed income — Nu infers what a payslip would say
Remittances as incomeCash & manual deposit rhythmPlatform earnings via Rollee / PalencaGrowth hub with a visible pathRewards on recargas & basics

Same terms as Track A once income is observed with confidence. Consent-first, an explicit exclusion list, and the estimate shown back to the customer.

Why one tier: two products would mean two roadmaps, two pricing committees and, inevitably, a worse deal for the customer with less paperwork — the outcome the mission exists to prevent. Same tier is also the simplest thing to defend in a fairness review.

The path — growth hub

Score the customer as if they had repaid on time; show the limit that unlocks; show the two actions that get there. A commitment, not a score.

Shipped in México, this segment: −1.43% default rate exposing the next limit 10 days pre-due (ITT, 95% CI −2.00 to −0.90). A plain loan-number ladder captured ~4/5 of it — ship the simple version first.

Why Nu can go deeper than anyone: aspiration is the acquisition channel — people want to bring Nu their money — and data is the underwriting channel. Risky segments get a clear path instead of a no.

Pricing that follows the data

Same yield budget. Pointed at the relationship, not the swipe.

1
Quote a spread, not a number.Brazil already quotes its box as 120% of CDI. México quotes "13% fija", so every cut is a headline. Align with group practice.
2
Gate the premium on income landing.One card purchase a month proves the customer is alive, not that they bank with us.
3
Cap on income, not a flat MX$25,000.Average balance is ~US$355; a flat cap binds only on the hot-money tail we least want to pay 13% for.
4
Progression buys retention cheaper than yield.The customer who is climbing is the customer who is repaying.
Budget-neutral at launch

Base tier = CETES, matched (today's 6.50%). Premium = CETES + 6.5 (today's 13%). Both tiers reproduce the published rates — what changes is who earns the premium, not how much premium is spent.

Cuenta Nu launched in May 2023 at 9% when Banxico sat at 11.25%. The base was never bought with rate — which is why it can be held without one.

Guardrail · credit

Default rate on observed-income lines vs. documented-income lines. Same tier is only defensible if the outcomes match.

Guardrail · fairness

Approval, limit and price parity by gender and state. ENIF shows a 10pp gender gap in formal credit — we should be closing it, not encoding it.
Risks

Where this breaks, and what I would do about it.

1
Balance sensitivity rises once a licensed competitor advertises against us. most likelyIt is low today because the base was won on brand. Instrument it by segment via a geographic holdout in the first six weeks — before anyone else is licensed.
2
The 11-day portability gap leaks.Name the date, show the steps, monitor the old account. It is the retention mechanic, not polish.
3
Bust-out on newly sized lines.Stage limits, short tenors, progression rather than jumps. Three populations: fraud, over-reached, and the ones who climb — the third has to outweigh the first two, and we measure that.
4
Proxies creep into the income model. reputationalExclusion by policy, outcome-parity testing, and a counterfactual you can safely show the customer. If you can't show it, the model is using something it shouldn't.
5
Data-partner coverage.Rollee / Palenca cover the platforms, not the tienda. Treat them as one signal among four, never the gate.
6
We are a supervised bank now.CNBV model-risk governance and CONDUSEF explainability apply from day one. Build the appeal path before the model, not after.
The moat

A rate is copyable in a week. A year of someone's income landing with you is not.

1 · The income graph

Every month of observed income makes the next decision better and the next competitor's worse. Proprietary, compounding, and it cannot be bought from a bureau.

2 · The loop is already built

Autopay, selectable due date, points into Cajitas, recargas. Competitors need to build the machine; Nu only needs to feed it.

3 · The licence window

Payroll hosting and IPAB cover, now. Mercado Pago and Klar are still queuing. Twelve months is a long head start on a habit.

4 · Aspiration

People bring Nu their money willingly. That is why Nu can go deeper into risky segments than an incumbent — the customer wants to be seen here.

5 · Switching cost that is a benefit

Leaving costs the customer their income-sized line and their payday-aligned autopay. It is not lock-in; it is a better deal that only works here.

6 · Cost to serve

The mass market is only profitable at Nu's cost structure. Incumbents cannot follow into the informal half even if they wanted to.

In one sentence

You are not short of deposits. You are short of visible income — and that is a product problem before it is a pricing one.

See the customer, price them fairly, show them the path. That is how a bank becomes loved — and how it becomes the one that wins.
