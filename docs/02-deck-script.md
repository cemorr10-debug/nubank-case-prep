## 4 · The core value proposition  ·  *3:00*

> ## *"La cuenta donde llega tu dinero."*
> **The account your money arrives in — not the one it sits in.**

```
   ANCHOR                 ENGINE                    MULTIPLIER
   Income lands here  →   Relationship-priced   →   Credit & protection
   nómina · remesas       yield, quoted as a        sized on income we
   plataformas            spread over CETES         can actually see
   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   fixes acquisition      fixes cost of funds       fixes the 35% LDR
```

**Say:** "The alternative proposition is 'the best rate in Mexico.' I'd argue against it for
two reasons. It's matchable in a week by any licensed competitor. And it selects for exactly
the customers we least want — the ones who leave for 50 basis points. **Income landing is a
habit. A rate is a price.** Habits are defensible; prices aren't."

## 5 · Mass-market bundles: two tiers, earned by behaviour  ·  *3:00*

| | **Nu Base** | **Nu Nómina** |
|---|---|---|
| How you qualify | any customer | recurring income lands at Nu — **documented *or* observed** |
| Yield | CETES + 1.5% | **CETES + 4.0%**, up to a balance cap |
| Credit | score-based, thin | line sized on observed income |
| Protection | optional | micro-cover included |
| What it costs us | low | **lower per peso of balance** |

**Four design decisions, and why:**
1. **Two tiers, not five.** Mass market punishes complexity. Every tier is a support call.
2. **The premium is earned by a behaviour, not a balance.** We pay up only for money that
   arrives with data and stickiness behind it.
3. **Cap the premium balance.** We're buying the relationship, not the treasury balance.
   Above the cap, the customer gets the base rate — and we stop overpaying for hot money.
4. **One tier, two doors.** Documented income and observed income reach *identical* terms.
   This is the whole inclusion argument, and it's also the simplest thing to defend in a
   fairness review — if the informal tier isn't a lower tier, there's no disparate outcome
   to explain.

## 7 · Pricing framework  ·  *3:00*

**Four moves:**

1. **Quote a spread, not a number.** "CETES + 4.00%", not "10.49%". As Banxico cuts, funding
   cost falls automatically and there's never a "Nu cut your rate" headline — because we
   didn't. *Risk to test: customers may anchor on absolute numbers, and the competitive
   comparison sites certainly do.*
2. **Price the relationship, not the balance.** Premium requires income landing; capped.
3. **Segment β — never blend it.** A blended β hides that the base is two populations:
   brand-acquired at β≈0 and rate-shoppers at β high. **Blended β makes every cut look safe
   until it suddenly isn't.**
4. **Retire "deposit growth" as a goal metric.** At 35% LDR it's a cost line wearing a growth
   metric's clothes. Replace with **deployable deposits** and **cost of funds vs. TIIE**.

**The trade-off I'd name out loud:** the rate is simultaneously our biggest acquisition asset
and our biggest cost line. That's precisely why I wouldn't cut the headline — I'd change what
it's quoted *against* and what it's *attached to*.

**The data I'd need to make the call — and would ask for in week one:**
- β **by segment**, from a geographic rate holdout. Not a blended number.
- LTV by acquisition source: yield-acquired vs. income-acquired cohorts
- Balance concentration — what share of the $5.7B sits in the top 5%? Tells us whether the
  premium cap even binds
- Attrition elasticity vs. the *competitor gap*, not vs. our own rate
- Marginal risk-adjusted credit yield by segment — is deployment actually accretive, or am I
  wrong about the whole thesis?

## 9 · Track A — *Trae tu nómina*  ·  *4:00*  ·  **PROTOTYPE**

- 22.76M IMSS formal jobs · ~36% of adults hold a payroll account · **~90% of those sit with
  a handful of big banks**
- **The wedge:** portability is the *worker's* legal right, banks cannot charge for it, and
  it completes in **11 business days**. No employer sale required.
- **Sell the credit line, not the rate.** The rate attracts the deposits we least want.
- **The metric is completed first salary landings.** Requests are vanity; the deposit
  arriving is the event that moves the balance sheet.
- **The risk is the 11-day gap** — a long silence after an irreversible-feeling decision.
  Naming a date, showing the steps and watching the old account *is* the retention mechanic.

**Say:** "The incumbent moat here is a corporate contract signed years ago. Portability makes
it irrelevant, because the decision belongs to the worker. This is the same consumer-pull
move Nu used against card incumbents in Brazil — and it's the one play that stops working
the day Mercado Pago is licensed."

## 10 · Track B — *Ingreso observado*  ·  *4:00*  ·  **PROTOTYPE**

- **55.1% of the workforce is informal — 33.1M people.** A formal-only strategy serves 44.6%
  of workers, in a country where only **37.3% of adults can access formal credit at all**.
- **Same tier. Different proof.** Not a lesser product for people with less paperwork.
- Signals: inbound rhythm · remittances (**US$30.76B in H1 2026**) · platform payouts · cash
  deposit patterns
- **The exclusion list is the product.** No device model, no location trail, no contacts, no
  social graph — every one of them predicts repayment, and every one is a proxy for class,
  region and ethnicity. Excluded **by policy**, and the customer is told so on the consent screen.
- **Show the estimate back to the customer**, with the reasoning and an appeal path.

**Say:** "I've built this underwriting spine before, at Tala, for exactly this customer. The
thing I'd carry over is the appeal path — it consistently produced better data than any
feature we added, because customers correct you precisely on the cases the model is least
sure about."

## 11 · Responsible AI — one rule, then the guardrails  ·  *2:00*

> ## Personalisation may only move a price in the customer's favour.
> AI decides **eligibility, limits, timing and experience.** It never decides that *this*
> customer will tolerate a worse price.

**Say:** "The risk in mass-market pricing AI isn't really a biased model. It's a model that
works exactly as designed. Price elasticity correlates with financial desperation — the
customers who won't shop are the customers who can't. A model optimising for
willingness-to-pay will systematically charge the most to the people least able to bear it.
That's not a bug you catch in monitoring. **It's the objective function.** So I'd rather
constrain the objective than audit the outcome."

**Guardrails:**
- Excluded features by policy, not by review
- Fairness tested on **outcomes** — approval, limit and price parity by gender and state.
  ENIF shows a **10pp gender gap in formal credit access (28.2% vs 18.3%)**. We should be
  closing that, not encoding it.
- Consent that costs nothing to decline — *if declining raises your price, it isn't consent*
- Estimate shown back + human appeal — contestability is what makes automated decisioning defensible
- We're a supervised bank now: model risk governance under CNBV, reasons explainable to CONDUSEF
- Champion/challenger gated on **fairness**, not only accuracy

## 13 · Alignment without authority  ·  *3:00*

**The scenario:** Card's roadmap collides with a bundle on a regulatory clock.

I wouldn't try to win the argument. I'd change what we're arguing about.

1. **Make it one P&L, not three roadmaps.** Core doesn't own Card or GBA — so Core should own
   the *currency*: a shared customer-level contribution view. Today the argument is "my
   roadmap versus yours," which no one can win on evidence. With a shared P&L it becomes
   "which sequence makes this customer worth more," which can actually be resolved.
2. **Trade assets, not favours.** I bring Card something they can't get anywhere else:
   verified income → better approval rates, higher limits, lower losses. They give me
   placement in their flows. That's a trade with a number attached, not a request.
3. **Never let the regulatory date be *my* deadline.** The moment it's my launch date, it's
   my problem and their favour. It's a shared compliance exposure — and I'd get that framing
   agreed with Legal *before* the conversation with Card, so it arrives as a fact rather
   than as my argument.
4. **Pre-agree the escalation path before you need it.** With Diego and the Card/GBA leads:
   what's mine, what's theirs, what goes up, and a time-box. Escalating without a pre-agreed
   path wins you one decision and costs you the relationship.
5. **Take the smaller ask.** If Card genuinely can't move, I ship the bundle without the card
   component and add it later. Shipping on time beats winning the argument.

## 14 · First six months, and the global conversation  ·  *3:00*

**Top 3, in this order:**

1. **Fix what we measure about deposits** *(weeks 1–6)* — segment β via geographic holdout;
   re-quote yield as a spread; retire deposit growth in favour of deployable deposits and
   cost of funds vs. TIIE. *First because it's nearly free, it's fast, and it's the evidence
   base for everything else — and because a new person with no credibility balance should
   spend the first month producing evidence, not asking for headcount.*
2. **Ship Track A to the cohort we can already detect** *(weeks 4–16)* — portability for
   customers whose payroll pattern is already in our data. *Second because it's the
   expiring opportunity.*
3. **Stand up Track B's consent + estimate spine** *(weeks 10–26)* — not the whole product:
   the consent flow, the estimator and the fairness harness, so H2 is an extension rather
   than a new build. *Third because it has the longest lead time, so it has to start early
   even though it lands late.*

**Representing Mexico globally:** the argument isn't "Mexico is different." It's
**"Mexico is the proving ground."**

Nu is accelerating internationalisation. Every new market will arrive exactly where Mexico is
now: newly licensed, low LDR, no income visibility. So I'd frame Mexico's asks as **global
optionality** — build the income-observation spine for Mexico and it becomes the template for
every subsequent licence.

**Asks framed as local exceptions lose. Asks framed as reusable platform capability win.**
And when they genuinely don't align, I'd say so early and take the no cleanly — spending
credibility on a fight I've already lost is the most expensive thing a local PM can do.

## 15 · Assumptions, and what I'd want on day one  ·  *1:30*

**What I assumed** (all in the model as sliders): 25% net credit yield · 28% LDR floor /
70% ceiling · 45% attach for full deployment · 8% income landing today · headline yield paid
on all balances (conservative) · β constant.

**Six questions I'd ask in week one:**
1. Is β blended, or do you have it by segment?
2. What share of the $5.7B sits in the top decile of balances?
3. What *is* the income-landing rate today? I assumed 8%.
4. What's the real risk-adjusted marginal credit yield?
5. What did the 15→11.5 cut do to **new acquisition**, not just retention?
6. **Why did retention hold** — brand, inertia, or switching cost? That answer changes the
   entire pricing strategy, and I don't think it can be inferred from outside.

**Closing line:** *"I've assumed a lot here and I've marked all of it. What I'd want to do in
week one is replace those assumptions with your data — and I'd expect at least one of them to
be wrong in a way that changes the plan. The thesis I'd defend is narrower than the deck: you
are not short of deposits, you are short of visible income, and that's a product problem
before it's a pricing one."*
