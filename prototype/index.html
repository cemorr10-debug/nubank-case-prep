How I would actually make the call

Three decisions this model produces, and the data I would need for each

  • Quote a spread, not a number. "13% fija" forces us to announce every cut — January's produced "ganarás menos por tus ahorros" (Expansión) and "pésimas noticias para clientes Nu" (Xataka). Against CETES, cost falls as Banxico moves with no headline. Needs: whether customers anchor on the number or on the ranking.
  • Fix the gate, not the size of the premium. The tier, the MX$25,000 cap and a behavioural gate all exist already. But one card purchase a month proves the customer is alive, not that they bank with us. Re-point the 13% at income landing, and scale the cap with income deposited. Needs: balance sensitivity by segment from a geographic holdout — the one number I would most want on day 1.
  • Stop treating deposit growth as a goal. At 35% LDR, deposits above what we can deploy are a cost line wearing a growth metric's clothes. Needs: LTV by acquisition source, split yield-acquired vs. income-acquired, which I would expect to differ by more than the rate does.
Where this model breaks

The honest limits

Balance sensitivity (β) is not constant. It is low today because the base was acquired on a brand promise, not a rate table; it rises sharply once a licensed competitor advertises against us. The model treats it as fixed, so it flatters any large cut — push the balance-sensitivity slider to 6 and the "cut the rate" preset stops working. That asymmetry is the point: a rate cut is a one-time harvest with rising risk, income visibility is a compounding asset.

The LDR ceiling is also an assumption, not a finding. It presumes underwriting quality holds as we deploy — which is exactly the thing to instrument before, not after.

22.76M formal workers. ~36% of adults hold a payroll account and ~90% of those sit with the big banks. Their moat is the employer relationship. Portabilidad de nómina routes around it entirely: it is the worker's legal right, banks cannot charge for it, and it completes in 11 business days. No HR sale required — which is the only reason a challenger can win this.

55.1% of Mexican workers are informal — 33.1 million people with no payslip to port. A formal-only strategy addresses under half the market and is off-mission for a company that exists for financial inclusion. So Track B earns the same tier through observed income instead of documented income. Same underwriting spine, different proof.
